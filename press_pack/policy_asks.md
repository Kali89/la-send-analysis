# Policy asks
### What the data says should happen

---

These asks follow directly from the findings. They are ordered by lead time — the longest-lead interventions are listed first because they are the ones most at risk of being delayed until it is too late.

---

## 1. Proactively engage the six named councils now

Bristol, Birmingham, Bromley, Lewisham, Staffordshire, and Central Bedfordshire show pre-crisis structural signatures on publicly available data with no formal DfE intervention in place. Several are already in collapse on timeliness — Central Bedfordshire's mean 20-week compliance of 26.5% is among the worst in the country.

**Ask**: DfE should confirm whether it is already engaging these councils, and on what basis. If not, proactive engagement should begin immediately. The Safety Valve programme was designed precisely for this situation.

---

## 2. Publish a public early-warning dashboard

The data to build a validated early-warning framework already exists and is published annually. The signals — absolute need-type growth, 20-week timeliness trend, tribunal appeal rate, independent placement spend as a share of DSG, and DSG balance — combine to predict collapse with AUC of up to 0.88 from data available three years in advance.

No such public framework currently exists. DfE monitoring has focused on proportional need-type shares (AUC ≈ 0.50, equivalent to chance) rather than absolute volumes.

**Ask**: DfE should publish an annual LA-level risk dashboard using the validated signals identified in this analysis, make the underlying data and methodology transparent, and commit to acting on it.

---

## 3. Publish annual LA-level need-type breakdowns

EHCP counts by primary need type (ASD, SEMH, SLCN, MLD) at LA level are currently available only for 2015/16–2019/20 and 2024/25. Four years of data (2020/21–2023/24) have no published LA-level breakdown — a significant gap for any council-level monitoring or early-warning system.

**Ask**: DfE should publish LA-level need-type data annually as part of the SEN2 statistical release.

---

## 4. Accelerate educational psychology workforce expansion

Educational psychologist (EP) services are a bottleneck in every stage of the EHCP process: assessment, plan quality, and annual review. Safety Valve councils already spend 41% more per EHCP on administration — the signature of caseworkers managing backlogs rather than delivering efficiently. EP spend per plan is flat, not reflecting efficiency but workforce scarcity.

Training an EP takes three to four years after a psychology degree. Expansion programmes announced in the 2023 SEND Improvement Plan are the right response but will take time to deliver.

**Ask**: Confirm that EP training place expansions are on track, and publish workforce projection data showing when the pipeline will reach the LA level.

---

## 5. Commission a national maintained special school capacity audit

Safety Valve LAs place 37% more children per 1,000 pupils in independent specialist schools than councils with no intervention (0.89 vs 0.65). Distance to the nearest maintained SEMH school is a significant predictor of independent spend (β = +0.016 per km, p = 0.003). The supply shortage is structural and geographically concentrated.

At £60,000–120,000 per independent placement per year, this is the primary driver of DSG deficits — and building a new maintained special school takes four to six years from planning to first pupils.

**Ask**: Publish a national audit of maintained special school capacity by need type and region, with projections of the gap against expected demand to 2030.
